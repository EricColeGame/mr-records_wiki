## What MR. RECORDS Is, According to Its Own Store Page

The [official Steam page for MR. RECORDS](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3053470/) presents it as an uplifting adventure rather than a pure note-matching rhythm game. The premise: George is an old dreamer who now runs the record shop he always wanted. Each vinyl he plays does not just produce music — it transports him into a world of memories and emotion. Inside those worlds, you jump, slide and move through psychedelic landscapes in time with the music. His dog, Buzz, accompanies him, and a colourful cast of customers and their stories fill out the shop side of the game.

That is the entire advertised identity. It reads as a rhythm-action platformer wrapped in a narrative frame, where the emotional hook is memory and the mechanical hook is moving on beat. Note the phrasing "in time with the music" — the page implies your movement is tied to rhythm, but it does not describe scoring systems, combo mechanics, judgement windows or difficulty settings. Anyone claiming otherwise is working from inference, not documentation.

## Why the Rhythm-Adventure Framing Is the Real Story

Plenty of rhythm games are abstract: you play songs, you chase scores. MR. RECORDS is advertised differently. The vinyl is not just a menu selection — it is a diegetic portal, and the shop is not just a hub — it is where George's customers and their stories live. That structure suggests two interleaved layers: a calm, character-driven shop where you meet people, and loud, kinetic memory worlds where you perform.

For a prospective player, this shapes what to expect. If you want a pure high-score chaser, the advertised emphasis on "memories and emotion" and "an uplifting adventure" signals something more narrative. If you want a story game, the jumping and sliding signals real mechanical demand. The honest answer is that the balance between those two halves has not been demonstrated publicly yet, and you should not assume it.
