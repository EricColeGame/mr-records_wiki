## Why the Rhythm-Platformer Blend Matters

Rhythm games and platformers solve different problems, and combining them changes what "skill" means. A pure rhythm game punishes you for missing a beat. A pure platformer punishes you for misjudging a jump. When the two merge, as the store description implies here, the beat typically dictates when a jump or slide is even possible.

That has practical consequences for anyone deciding whether to wishlist it:

- **Musical taste is a real factor.** If the soundtrack does not land, the levels will not either, because the level design is built on it.
- **Timing tolerance matters more than reflexes.** Players who enjoy rhythm games often prefer forgiving windows on a first pass; players who do not may find the movement restrictive.
- **The memory framing suggests variety.** Psychedelic memory worlds give a developer license to change visual rules between levels, which is common in this genre.

None of that is confirmed by a track list or a difficulty setting, because neither is public. It is reasoning from the genre and the description, not a promise about how the game plays.
